A studio booking is not just a date on the calendar. It carries client details, event locations, package commitments, payment expectations, crew needs, and delivery promises. When those details live across chat threads and sheets, every update becomes a small risk.

## Start with one booking record

Create the booking first, then attach the moving parts to it: clients, participants, event dates, shoots, package value, notes, and status. This gives the team one place to check before making a promise.

## Split events from shoots

A wedding, event, or production can include multiple dates and multiple shoot types. Keeping those shoots attached to the booking helps coordinators assign crew and track venues without losing the bigger client context.

## Keep finance close to operations

Payment schedules, received amounts, invoices, receipts, and expenses should belong to the booking they came from. That makes follow-up factual instead of awkward.

## Review daily

A good studio workflow answers three questions every morning: what is upcoming, what is overdue, and who owns the next step.
